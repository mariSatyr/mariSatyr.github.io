Peaks & Valleys

Explore six functions two ways: hunt for their local and absolute extrema, or map out exactly where each one is increasing, decreasing, or constant. Some have hills that aren't really the tallest, some stop at a wall, and one has a hole where the graph almost — but doesn't quite — get there.

Quick definitions
Local maximum — the top of a hill: higher than the points right around it, even if another hill is taller elsewhere.
Local minimum — the bottom of a valley: lower than the points right around it.
Absolute maximum — the single highest point on the entire graph.
Absolute minimum — the single lowest point on the entire graph.
Boundary point — an endpoint of the domain. It can be a local max/min too, if the curve only moves one direction away from it.
Domain & range — domain is every allowed input (x); range is every output (y) the function actually produces.
Increasing interval — as x increases, y increases too: the graph rises as you move left to right.
Decreasing interval — as x increases, y decreases: the graph falls as you move left to right.
Constant interval — y stays the same as x changes: the graph is flat.
