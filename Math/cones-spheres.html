Why the ⅓ and the ⅔ show up

CONE vs CYLINDER

Fill a cylinder of radius r and height h, then pour that water into a cone with the same radius and height. It takes exactly three cones' worth to fill the cylinder — which is why the cone's volume formula carries that ⅓ out front.

SPHERE vs CYLINDER

Now wrap a cylinder tightly around a sphere, so its radius and height both equal 2r. The sphere takes up exactly ⅔ of that cylinder's volume — a result Archimedes considered his finest, and reportedly asked to have carved on his tombstone.
