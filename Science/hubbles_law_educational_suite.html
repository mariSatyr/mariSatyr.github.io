Mathematical Formulation

Hubble's Law Equation:

$$v = H_0 \cdot d$$

Where $v$ is the recessional radial velocity of the galaxy in km/s, $d$ is the proper distance in Megaparsecs ($\text{Mpc}$), and $H_0$ is Hubble's Constant.

Calculating the Age of the Universe ($t_H$):

$$t_H = \frac{1}{H_0} \approx \frac{1}{70\ \text{km/s/Mpc}} \approx 13.9\ \text{Billion Years}$$

Assuming a linear expansion rate since the Big Bang, $1/H_0$ (the Hubble Time) provides an approximate upper bound on cosmic age. Converting units: $1\ \text{Mpc} \approx 3.086 \times 10^{19}\ \text{km}$.

Redshift ($\mathbf{z}$) & Spectral Lines

Galaxies do not move *through* space; rather, cosmic space itself stretches. This stretches passing photon wavelengths ($\lambda$), shifting absorption lines toward the red end of the spectrum.
