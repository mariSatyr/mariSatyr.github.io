Understanding Hubble's Law & Redshift

1

What is Redshift?

When light travels from distant galaxies, the expansion of space stretches the light's wavelength toward the red end of the electromagnetic spectrum. By measuring emission lines (like Hydrogen Alpha), astronomers determine how fast a galaxy is receding ($v = z \cdot c$).

2

Why Distant Objects Move Faster

Space itself is expanding uniformly everywhere. A galaxy twice as far away has twice as much expanding space between it and Earth, causing it to appear to move away at twice the speed. This is analogous to spots on an expanding balloon.

3

Calculating the Age of the Universe

If all galaxies started expanding from a single point, the inverse of Hubble's constant ($1 / H_0$) gives the approximate time since expansion began (Hubble Time $t_H$). Converting Mpc to km yields the cosmic age in years.

Unit Conversion Tip: 1 Megaparsec (Mpc) = $3.086 \times 10^{19}$ km. Thus, $1 / H_0$ in seconds $\approx 3.086 \times 10^{19} / H_0$, which equals $\approx 977.8 / H_0$ billion years.
