Distances are in AU (1 AU = Earth–Sun distance). Time is in Earth years.

Law 1: orbits are ellipses

The Sun sits at one focus. The two distances from the planet to the foci always add up to the same total, 2a.

Law 2: equal areas in equal times

The pink wedge is what the planet sweeps in one-eighth of an orbit. Its area stays the same wherever it is, so the planet speeds up when close to the Sun.

Law 3: period and distance

T² divided by a³ is the same for every planet around the same star. A heavier star makes the number smaller.

Try this

  1. Double the Sun’s mass and keep everything else the same. Does the planet take more or less time to orbit? Check T in Law 3.
  2. Slide the planet’s mass from Earth-size up to the maximum. What happens to the period? Turn on the center of mass view to see the Sun move.
  3. Raise the eccentricity to 0.8 and compare the speed at the closest and farthest points.
  4. Change the distance a. How does T² ÷ a³ change?
